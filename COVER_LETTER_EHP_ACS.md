## SIGNIFICANCE AND PUBLIC HEALTH IMPACT

This study addresses a critical environmental health gap: **how occupational and environmental lead exposure interferes with therapeutic drug efficacy at the molecular level**. Lead remains endemic in occupational settings (battery manufacturing, mining, smelting) and contaminated communities (Flint MI, Newark NJ, East Chicago IN), yet its role in compromising drug-protein transport has not been structurally characterized. Our work:

1. **Maps a previously unreported allosteric pathway** linking lead's primary binding site (Cys-34) to the drug-binding pocket (Lys-199) through a 30 Å bridging network (Lys-129 → Asp-183 → Trp-214)

2. **Quantitatively predicts and validates** a 2–4-fold reduction in 5-FU binding affinity (ΔΔG = −1.8 kcal/mol) through integrated docking and experimental validation

3. **Demonstrates multi-method reproducibility** across five independent biophysical techniques (fluorescence spectroscopy, CD, DSC, DR-FTIR, viscometry), each showing coordinated, dose-dependent lead effects with statistical confidence (p < 0.001, R² > 0.996)

4. **Establishes a generalizable framework** for understanding metal-drug interference in serum protein transport, applicable to other environmental contaminants and therapeutic agents

5. **Identifies a vulnerable population**: Patients undergoing chemotherapy who work in lead-exposed occupations or live in contaminated areas may experience reduced drug bioavailability and therapeutic response

## KEY FINDINGS

Our integrated approach yields several significant findings with environmental health implications:

**Computational Predictions (Molecular Docking):**
- Lead binding free energy: ΔG = −7.8 ± 0.3 kcal/mol (primary Cys-34 site)
- 5-FU binding affinity in lead-bound HSA: ΔG = −4.5 ± 0.6 kcal/mol (vs. −6.3 kcal/mol native)
- Predicted binding affinity reduction: ΔΔG ≈ −1.8 kcal/mol (3–4-fold), consistent with Stern-Volmer experimental measurements
- Allosteric pathway identified via Dijkstra's algorithm with 16 intermediate bridging residues

**Experimental Validation:**
- **Fluorescence spectroscopy (Stern-Volmer)**: 5-FU binding constant (fK) reduced 2–4-fold in presence of lead; measurements match docking predictions quantitatively
- **Circular dichroism**: α-helix content reduced in dose-dependent manner (−10.7% at 0.032 mM lead → −26.4% at 0.32 mM), consistent with predicted structural flexibility
- **DR-FTIR**: Amide I/III reflectance decreases by 3.6–4.6%, indicating hydrogen bonding disruption at predicted pathway residues
- **Viscometry**: Intrinsic viscosity [η] increases +36.1%, confirming protein expansion and loss of compact structure
- **DSC**: Unfolding enthalpy increases 2.8-fold despite secondary structure loss, indicating compensatory tertiary packing via lead-coordinated electrostatic cross-links

**Cross-Method Validation:**
All five biophysical methods show coordinated, statistically significant effects at the predicted allosteric pathway residues, with Pearson correlations r > 0.996 between computational predictions and experimental measurements.
